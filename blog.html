Ideas for preparing for birth, finding your voice, and making room for care after your baby arrives.

What does a birth doula do?

When we meet before your birth, I’ll ask what helps you feel at ease, what you’re looking forward to, and what’s been on your mind. We can talk through your birth preferences and write down questions you want to bring to your care team.

During labor, support can look like a quiet reminder to relax your shoulders, helping you try a different position, or making sure your partner knows how to help. Your medical team handles your clinical care. My role is to offer practical and emotional support and make space for you to ask questions and share your preferences.

If you’re wondering what that could look like for your family, let’s talk on a free 20-minute intro call. Your partner is welcome to join.

Three questions to start your birth preferences conversation

A birth preferences list can help the people supporting you understand what matters to you. Start with a few sentences you’d feel comfortable sharing with your partner, doula, or care team.

  1. What helps me feel grounded? Think about quiet, music, a familiar voice, or someone reminding you to take things one moment at a time.
  2. How do I like to get information? You might want a plain-language explanation, a chance to ask questions, or a moment to talk with your support person when time allows.
  3. What would help my support person feel ready? Pick one thing they can do, like keeping track of questions or helping you get comfortable between conversations.

Bring your answers to a prenatal visit and ask how your preferences might fit the setting where you plan to give birth.

Plan for your first days home, too

While we’re talking about your birth, I like to ask what would help you feel cared for once you’re home. Who could bring dinner? Who could come by so you can rest? If you’re unsure how to ask, we can find words that feel like yours.

You get to decide when you’re ready for visitors. When someone offers to come over, it’s okay to give them one thing that would help. You might say, “I’d love to see you. Could you bring lunch when you come? I’d like to keep the visit short so I can rest afterward.”

Start with one person and one thing they could take off your plate. You deserve to be cared for while you care for your baby. We can talk through the rest together.

You don’t have to have it all figured out. One small step is enough for today.
